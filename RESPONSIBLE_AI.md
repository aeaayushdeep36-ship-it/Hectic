# Responsible AI

Hectic is an independent student project submitted to the AI Agents for Founders
Challenge. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced by HETIC.

This document answers the seven questions the brief asks of any product that uses
a language model. It is written to be checked, not to reassure.

---

## 1. What the model does

Three language steps plus two deterministic functions. A1's prompt is versioned
in `agents/prompts/`; A2 and A3 use schema-bound inline prompts declared next to
their agents.

| Step | Input | Output | Model class |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 `claim-formaliser` | one sentence of belief | population, predicate, kind, claim type, ambiguity flags | small |
| A2 `relevance-tagger` | redacted notes + claim predicate | one batch of `bearsOn` and polarity tags per claim | smallest |
| A3 `constraint-explainer` | an already-computed verdict | two factual sentences | small |
| A4 `statement-composer` | the locked ledger | method, coverage, findings, limitations prose | none (pure) |
| G1 `input-guard` | raw cells and notes | redacted text + injection flag | none (regex) |

Runtimes are free-tier or open-weight only: Gemini `gemini-flash-lite-latest`, Groq
`qwen/qwen3.8-27b`, Ollama `qwen2.5:7b`, optional OpenRouter
`google/gemma-4-31b-it:free`, and an in-repo deterministic mock. The mock is the
default in the shipped configuration and needs no key. No secret is stored in the
repository.

## 2. What the model cannot do

It cannot produce a level, a count, a score, a threshold, a binding constraint
or a verdict. This is not a policy statement; it is structural:

- The A1 output schema has no numeric field (`lib/schemas.ts`).
- A2 emits only three string/boolean fields and is human-overridable.
- A3 rejects any generated digit that does not appear in the deterministic text.
- A4 receives a locked ledger and is checked against it.
- `engine/` has zero imports from any provider, from React, or from the database.
  `npm run engine:boundary` enforces this; swapping providers leaves
  `finding_hash` byte-identical (automated test, `tests/store.test.ts`).

## 3. Known failure modes

| Failure | Direction of error | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| A1 widens the population (e.g. "practice managers" → "healthcare professionals") | Makes the sample look *more* adequate | Mandatory human confirmation; every field editable; ambiguity flags shown |
| A2 finds relevance everywhere | Inflates counts | `bears_on: false` prompt discipline; visible tags; human override stored with `overridden_by_user` |
| A3/A4 invent a number | Misleading artefact | Numeric guard falls back to deterministic template |
| Person names in free text | PII reaches a provider | Guard redacts named fields, two-token names, naming-context single names, emails, phones, IBANs and URLs before storage and egress; aliases still required; residual risk disclosed in §9 |
| Injection in notes or claim text | Tag pollution only | Pattern screen, JSON encoding, structural separation, and no numeric field to corrupt |

## 4. Who is affected

The founder's interviewees — people who never heard of this product and did not
consent to AI processing. Mitigations: offline operation by default, no account,
no telemetry, aliases instead of names, redaction before any provider call, and
notes excluded from the export entirely. If Gemini, Groq or OpenRouter is
configured, redacted notes leave the machine and that provider becomes a
processor; the founder makes that choice explicitly and the key is never
committed.

## 5. What the tool must not be used for

- As legal, financial or investment advice.
- As a substitute for a research method.
- To certify evidence the founder knows to be fabricated.
- As a "market size" or "TAM" generator. Hectic audits discovery evidence only.

## 6. AI-assisted development

The engine, thresholds, database schema, API routes, UI components and tests
were written with AI assistance and reviewed against the specifications in
`docs/`. The research in `research/` was conducted with web fetches and Python
batteries, also AI-assisted. All citations were fetched from primary sources or
publisher pages; the Pattyn paper was read in full. No part of the product was
generated by a third-party service at runtime without disclosure above.

## 7. Limits of the certification

Printed on the artefact itself, not buried here:

> A level means "the strongest evidence type present, supported by this many
> independent organisations" — not "the conclusion is true". Pattyn et al. is an
> exploratory study of 16 startups, not a law. Saturation figures transfer from
> other research settings. The Wilson interval assumes random sampling that a
> founder's network never gives; it is a best case, and true uncertainty is wider.

The full falsification audit of the original scoring ladder is in
`research/03-engine-findings.md`.

## 8. Bias assessment

This section names the ways a language model could tilt an audit, the direction
of the error, and the control that limits it. It is deliberately written as a
list of *known* biases, not as a claim of neutrality.

| Bias | How it shows up here | Direction of error | Mitigation | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Population widening** — small chat models expand a narrow role to a familiar category ("practice managers" → "healthcare professionals") | A1 formalisation | Makes the sample look **more** adequate than it is | The proposal is never accepted automatically; every field requires human confirmation; ambiguity flags are shown; the population is deliberately narrowed to a substring of the founder's own words | A founder who clicks through without reading can still accept a widened population |
| **Relevance inflation** — the model treats politeness, a single shared word, or silence as evidence | A2 tagging | Inflates `support` | One batched prompt per claim, `bearsOn: false` default, two distinct predicate terms in the deterministic fallback, visible and overridable tags with the producer recorded | Prompt injection can pollute tags; it cannot change a verdict, because the engine reads only the founder-confirmed roster |
| **Authority bias** — confident prose makes a shaky verdict feel settled | A3 explanation, A4 statement | Overstates certainty | No single score is emitted; the verdict is always `level · support · precision · binding`; the statement prints limitations and the Wilson caveat on the artefact itself | Judges and founders may still read `SUPPORTED` as "validated" |
| **Sampling bias** — Wilson intervals assume random sampling; founder recruitment is network/convenience | Engine presentation | Understates uncertainty | The interval is labelled a **best case** in the statement and in `docs/06-testing.md`; network share and channel count are diagnostics, never gates | A reader who ignores the label will under-estimate uncertainty |
| **Language and name bias** — name detection must work across naming conventions without destroying ordinary prose | G1 guard, A1 fallback | Mixed: false redaction of ordinary prose, or missed PII for unusual conventions | Guard combines named-field hints, explicit naming contexts, title/particle support and Unicode-aware name tokens; role prose, acronyms and sentence openers are preserved; every redaction is visible in the audit log | Founder-supplied aliases cannot be verified; see §9 |
| **Model variance** — the same prompt can produce different prose across providers | A1–A3 | Presentation drift only | Schema validation, tolerant-but-validated JSON parsing, numeric guard on A3, and a provider-swap test asserting byte-identical finding hashes **and** identical fallback behaviour | Prose quality on hosted models is still human-review work; `docs/eval-gate.md` scores only the deterministic path, while `docs/eval-live.md` records real-provider repetitions and failures |

The controls are structural where possible and human where not. No model is in a
position to invent a number: the schemas contain no numeric verdict fields and
`engine/` has no provider imports. `npm run engine:boundary` enforces the latter.

## 9. GDPR redaction coverage and residual risk

`lib/guard.ts` now applies strict redaction before storage and before any provider
call. Automated regressions in `tests/remediation.test.ts` cover:

- two-token names with titles, hyphens and particles (`Priya Raman`,
  `Dr. Anne-Marie de Groot`, `José da Silva`, `Élodie Müller`);
- single-token names in naming contexts (`Priya said X`, `called Priya about
  the pilot`) while preserving ordinary prose such as *"Most managers said X"*;
- lowercase names supplied through explicit fields (`name: adi priyo described
  the gap`, `contact = juan pérez mentioned budget`);
- e-mail addresses, national and international phone numbers, IBANs and URLs,
  alongside the existing formula and injection screens.

The detector uses field hints, naming contexts and Unicode-aware name tokens
rather than a blanket capitalised-word rule; that avoids turning ordinary role
prose and sentence openers into false positives.

Remaining residual risk is limited and disclosed rather than hidden:

1. Aliases in the `alias` column are founder-supplied; Hectic cannot verify that
   they are truly anonymous.
2. A name written with no naming context, no title and no conventional capital
   or particle pattern can still be missed.
3. Redaction reduces but does not by itself establish GDPR compliance; lawful
   basis, retention and data-subject rights remain the founder's obligations.

Mitigations: the UI asks for aliases, raw notes are excluded from the printable
export, processing is local unless a remote provider is explicitly configured,
there is no telemetry, and the audit log records redactions and flags only.

## 10. Remote processing disclosure — Gemini, Groq and OpenRouter

Selecting a hosted provider sends **redacted** notes and claim text to that
provider's OpenAI-compatible endpoint: Gemini (`gemini-flash-lite-latest`), Groq
(`qwen/qwen3.8-27b`), or optional OpenRouter (`google/gemma-4-31b-it:free`).
Each API key is read only from the server environment, is never sent to the
browser, and is absent from the repository. No account or payment detail is held
by Hectic.

Each hosted runtime is a third-party processor for that request. The mitigation
chain is unchanged: the input guard strips email, phone, IBAN, URLs, formulas,
likely person names and injection strings before egress; the response is
schema-checked with Zod; A3 re-checks every generated digit; and the verdict
itself is computed locally by `engine/` before any call. Free-tier rate limits
are handled by batching, bounded retry with jittered backoff, and an LRU cache.
If the service is unavailable or over quota, the deterministic template fallback
runs and the reason is logged — the audit still completes.

`npm run eval:live` is the repeatability harness for this runtime: it exercises
each configured provider ten times by default, records every fallback and
schema failure in `docs/eval-live.md`, and refuses to claim the iterative gate
until at least two real models have been run.
